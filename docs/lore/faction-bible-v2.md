# WORLDSCOLIDE — Faction Bible v2

> Internal faction reference for writers, artists, quest designers, combat designers, and narrative planning.
> This document follows the revised canon in **WORLDSCOLIDE — Canon Revision v2** and treats each civilization as a complete worldview, not just a combat archetype.

---

# How to Use This Document

Each faction entry is written to support:
- **Lore writing** — worldview, myths, internal contradictions, social structure
- **Character writing** — archetypes, speech tone, emotional logic, common conflicts
- **Art direction** — materials, shapes, motifs, silhouette language, color direction
- **Quest design** — internal politics, faction tensions, knowledge gaps, reveal hooks
- **Gameplay identity** — combat fantasy and strategic role

Each faction should feel:
- morally legible, but not morally pure
- culturally specific, but internally diverse
- rooted in the same world, but shaped by different answers to its central wound

---

# Shared World Principle

All five major civilizations are responses to the same historical trauma.
They do not merely fight over territory.
They fight over **how humanity should survive inside a damaged world**.

Each faction preserves one distorted response to the ancient collapse:
- **Linh Sơn** — preserve balance
- **Đế Quốc** — impose order
- **Thiên Lữ** — carry living memory
- **The Eliza** — sanctify history into doctrine
- **The Scavenger** — survive by profiting from ruins

No faction holds the entire truth.
Each holds one part of it, and each misunderstands another part.

# 1. LINH SƠN

## 1.1 Core Fantasy

**The people of mountain blood and living stone.**
A civilization that treats land, ancestry, body, and Ether as parts of the same breathing system.
Linh Sơn endures where others expand, preach, or consume.

They are not simply “nature people.”
They are a people of discipline, memory in the flesh, and sacred restraint.

## 1.2 Core Theme

> The world survives only if power remains bound to balance.

Linh Sơn believes strength without reverence becomes desecration.
They do not separate body from land, or inheritance from duty.

## 1.3 Public Image

Other civilizations often see Linh Sơn as:
- stubborn mountain traditionalists
- protectors of old sacred lands
- physically resilient and difficult to conquer
- secretive keepers of Ether-rich territories
- a people too attached to ritual and ancestry

## 1.4 Internal Truth

Linh Sơn does not merely worship the land.
They believe the land is **alive with continuity**.
Ether is not a neutral resource to them; it is bound into long-vein systems, burial lines, memory sites, and mountain pressure.

They are among the earliest descendants of communities that encountered Ether directly in old strata and ruins.
This gave rise to both their spiritual traditions and their altered bloodlines.

Their greatest fear is not invasion alone.
It is **imbalance** — the kind of extraction, rupture, or profanation that permanently destabilizes a region.

## 1.5 What They Get Right

- Ether has limits.
- The land can be wounded in ways that machines do not immediately measure.
- not all progress is worth its cost
- memory and ritual can preserve truths that data systems lose

## 1.6 What They Get Wrong

- they can become too protective and too closed
- they often hoard knowledge in ritualized forms
- they may allow suffering in the present to avoid violating ancestral law
- they can mistake inherited forms for eternal truth

## 1.7 Society and Social Structure

Linh Sơn is organized around **clan-lines, oath-circles, and land-guard responsibilities**.
Their authority is layered rather than centralized.

### Key social bodies
- **Elders of Vein and Breath** — ritual keepers and interpreters of long-memory
- **Cliff Wardens** — defenders of mountain approaches, caves, and sacred sites
- **Forge-Hearth Houses** — smith lineages that rework ancient metal into ceremonial and martial forms
- **River and Mist Watchers** — scouts who monitor changes in local Ether flow, beast migration, and intrusion signs
- **Tattoo Keepers** — not mere artists, but recorders of vows, wounds, lineage, and state of awakening

Authority in Linh Sơn is earned through endurance, service, and proven alignment with ancestral obligation.
They do not admire charisma as much as reliability.

## 1.8 Daily Life

Linh Sơn daily life should feel grounded, labor-intensive, communal, and sacred without being exoticized.

Common features:
- terraced settlements on cliff edges or within mountain chambers
- suspended bridges, carved poles, rainwater cisterns, and shrine-stones
- training grounds built into practical labor zones
- ritual meals tied to season, hunt, and ancestral observance
- children learning endurance, climbing, and oral lineage from an early age

Their homes should feel built **with terrain**, not imposed against it.

## 1.9 Relationship to Ether

Linh Sơn sees Ether as:
- blood in the bones of the world
- dangerous if torn out too greedily
- spiritually resonant
- connected to altered inheritance and awakened lineage

They know more than they publicly admit about unstable Ether zones.
They are especially sensitive to shifts in land-tone, beast behavior, root blackening, mineral glow, and dream disturbances.

## 1.10 Relationship to The Mist

They view The Mist partly as an outer force and partly as a sign that the world has already been wounded.
Some elders teach that The Mist does not merely advance from the sea — it also seeps through broken veins beneath the earth.

This makes Linh Sơn unusually alert to subterranean incursions, collapsed vaults, and ancient buried structures.

## 1.11 Relationship to Donny Ivan

Linh Sơn does not preserve a clear historical memory of Ivan as a political figure.
Instead, they retain fragments of him as:
- a breaker of deep order
- a king-who-burned-the-roots
- a face tied to unnatural fire and severed land

They understand his legacy through consequence, not ideology.

## 1.12 Relationship to Minh Yun

Linh Sơn likely preserves the most spiritualized traces of Minh Yun.
He may survive in their memory not as a scientist, but as:
- the Listener Below Stone
- the Man Who Heard Light Under the Earth
- the First One Who Touched the Veins and Warned of Their Hunger

## 1.13 Aesthetic Direction

### Shape language
- broad, stable silhouettes
- layered wraps and practical armor
- thick vertical supports
- carved geometry inspired by mountain lines, bronze drum rhythm, river curves, bird forms

### Materials
- wood, stone, bamboo, woven fiber, leather, bronze, blackened recovered steel
- polished bone and river shell as sacred accents
- restrained Ether in ritual contexts rather than flashy display

### Color direction
- earth white
- wet clay brown
- bronze gold
- indigo
- weathered green
- ember-orange as rare ritual accent

## 1.14 Combat Identity

### Strategic role
- Defender / Counter-holder / Terrain tank

### Combat fantasy
- unmovable when wounded
- rooted stance
- pressure through endurance
- punishing overextension
- fighting like a mountain that suddenly strikes back

### Design logic
They should not feel slow in a dull way.
They should feel **committed, grounded, and punishing to dislodge**.

## 1.15 Internal Conflicts

Good quests and character stories should draw from these tensions:
- elders who hide dangerous truths about Ether collapse
- younger warriors who want controlled exchange with the outside world
- smith lineages who disagree over whether ancient metal should be reforged or buried
- clan obligations versus personal grief or love
- preserving sacred sites versus sheltering refugees in time of crisis

## 1.16 Character Archetypes

- the scarred cliff captain who never abandons a pass
- the young tattoo-bearer marked by an unstable awakening
- the elder who can sense vein-disturbance through touch
- the forge heir who studies forbidden old-world metallurgy
- the ritual envoy sent unwillingly into outer diplomacy

## 1.17 Writing Tone

Linh Sơn dialogue should feel:
- concise
- deliberate
- grounded in bodily metaphor, ancestry, weight, debt, weather, and stone

They should not sound mystical in an airy fantasy way.
They should sound like people who learned spirituality through labor, survival, and inheritance.

## 1.18 Common Sayings

- “A cut hill remembers the blade.”
- “Stand first. Speak later.”
- “Roots do not shout.”
- “A light taken wrongly burns twice.”

## 1.19 Quest Hooks

- a sacred mountain vein is weakening because an old buried relay beneath it has reactivated
- a clan tomb contains pre-collapse metalwork bearing Minh Yun’s marks
- a Linh Sơn elder knows the first warning about Ether exhaustion but refuses to reveal it
- a border village asks whether refugees from a Mist-broken coast should be accepted at the cost of violating taboo land

# 2. ĐẾ QUỐC

## 2.1 Core Fantasy

**The civilization of engineered order.**
The inheritors of technical memory, strategic thinking, electrical infrastructure, glass-steel ambition, and disciplined expansion.

They do not remember the old world through nostalgia.
They remember it through systems.

## 2.2 Core Theme

> If the world is damaged, then it must be measured, rebuilt, and governed.

Đế Quốc believes civilization survives by coordination, hierarchy, infrastructure, and applied intelligence.
Their moral danger is the belief that what can be organized can therefore be owned.

## 2.3 Public Image

Other factions often see Đế Quốc as:
- civilized but domineering
- efficient, disciplined, and highly capable
- technologically superior and politically manipulative
- dangerously confident in systems and extraction
- the faction most likely to turn survival into administration

## 2.4 Internal Truth

Đế Quốc is not a direct time-displaced remnant of the 21st century.
It is the descendant of centuries of broken transmission: archives, diagrams, engineering rituals, machine practices, administrative doctrine, and institutional habits inherited from post-collapse powers linked to the Republic Empire.

They know more than most about old systems, but even they work from fragments.
Much of what they call knowledge is actually **stabilized inheritance**, not full understanding.

## 2.5 What They Get Right

- scale matters; survival cannot rely on sentiment alone
- infrastructure is civilization
- technical literacy preserves worlds
- Ether must be studied, not merely feared or worshipped
- disciplined cooperation can prevent mass death

## 2.6 What They Get Wrong

- they overestimate their right to manage everything
- they often confuse control with wisdom
- extraction is too easily justified as necessity
- they can erase living cultures in the name of efficiency
- they risk accelerating Ether decline while trying to solve it

## 2.7 Society and Social Structure

Đế Quốc should feel like a fusion of bureaucracy, engineering tradition, military doctrine, and civic spectacle.

### Key institutions
- **The Rail Ministries** — transport, industrial coordination, logistics
- **The Glass Academies** — engineering, optics, applied Ether science, tactical theory
- **The Conduct Corps** — military officers and infrastructure guardians
- **The Arc Bureaus** — power regulation, Ether grid management, emergency stabilization
- **The Civic Chambers** — administration, law, census, diplomatic registration

Status comes from competence, rank, achievement, technical mastery, lineage of service, and political usefulness.

## 2.8 Daily Life

Daily life in Đế Quốc should feel synchronized, urbanizing, and procedural.

Common features:
- scheduled transit and regimented work cycles
- industrial districts, station towers, signal structures, civic plazas
- clean-lined apartments for officials and cramped worker quarters near infrastructure
- public announcements, inspection culture, permit systems
- schools emphasizing literacy, arithmetic, mechanics, and state history

Their world should feel bright with electricity but shadowed by extraction.

## 2.9 Relationship to Ether

Đế Quốc sees Ether as:
- the foundation of modern civilization
- a measurable strategic substrate
- a resource that must be disciplined into grids, engines, weapons, and urban systems
- the key to defending humanity from outer collapse

They are among the most capable at large-scale Ether application.
They are also among the most dangerous in terms of overuse.

## 2.10 Relationship to The Mist

Đế Quốc treats The Mist as a problem to map, predict, fortify against, and eventually overcome.
They lead many frontier installations and anti-intrusion projects.

Yet their confidence may blind them to the fact that reality destabilization cannot always be solved by engineering alone.

## 2.11 Relationship to Donny Ivan

Đế Quốc does not remember Ivan cleanly.
He exists in different registers:
- as a half-legendary founder of hard transition
- as a figure buried beneath institutional myth
- as a shadow in the oldest technical doctrines
- as a dangerous political ancestor that many prefer not to name directly

Some revisionists within Đế Quốc may try to recover him as a ruthless but necessary architect.
Others see him as the first great distortion of state power.

## 2.12 Relationship to Minh Yun

Minh Yun survives most strongly in Đế Quốc through:
- unnamed theories
- inherited machine designs
- notation systems
- marginal annotations in old technical archives
- sealed research vaults deemed too dangerous for general access

A major internal tension is whether rediscovering his original work would save the continent or destroy it faster.

## 2.13 Aesthetic Direction

### Shape language
- vertical towers, rails, braces, segmented armor, layered coats
- elegant machinery, mechanical symmetry, civic geometry
- lenses, coils, frames, conduits, articulated gloves

### Materials
- glass, steel, brass, ceramic, enamel, lacquered wood, conductive composites
- controlled Ether light in chambers, cables, sealed devices

### Color direction
- coal black
- steel gray
- ivory white
- electric blue
- brass gold
- warning amber in industrial spaces

## 2.14 Combat Identity

### Strategic role
- Mid-range tactician / control support / precision pressure

### Combat fantasy
- coordinated timing
- positioning advantage
- marked targets
- power buildup through discipline
- battlefield superiority through planning, not brute force

They should feel like a civilization that turns order into force.

## 2.15 Internal Conflicts

- reformers versus hardline expansionists
- engineers who see Ether as finite versus officers who demand more output
- ethical disputes over experimentation and awakening research
- urban elites versus frontier labor corps
- archival truth versus state-approved history

## 2.16 Character Archetypes

- the brilliant academy engineer hiding data about Ether decline
- the officer who believes conquest is the only way to secure the frontier
- the station administrator torn between law and mercy
- the arc technician who has seen too many grid failures to trust the system
- the civic historian who starts finding traces of Ivan where there should be none

## 2.17 Writing Tone

Đế Quốc speech should be:
- articulate
- controlled
- precise
- mildly formal, even when emotional
- full of systems language, standards, efficiency, consequence, and public responsibility

## 2.18 Common Sayings

- “A city is a promise made visible.”
- “Order is mercy at scale.”
- “Measure before belief.”
- “Failure spreads faster than fire.”

## 2.19 Quest Hooks

- an Ether rail project threatens to drain a neighboring stable zone dry
- sealed Academy records reveal Minh Yun warned against current extraction methods
- a frontier defense station reports impossible distortions that standard instruments cannot classify
- an ambitious minister wants to recover banned doctrines linked to Ivan’s era

# 3. THIÊN LỮ

## 3.1 Core Fantasy

**The wandering keepers of living memory.**
A people who travel with stars, songs, caravans, beasts, and encoded histories.
They do not treat truth as something stored in walls.
They treat it as something carried, sung, repeated, and corrected by movement.

## 3.2 Core Theme

> What must survive should remain alive, not imprisoned.

Thiên Lữ believes memory dies when it is fixed too rigidly.
A truth worth keeping must still be able to move through people, routes, voices, and generations.

## 3.3 Public Image

Other civilizations often see Thiên Lữ as:
- mysterious wanderers
- prophets, traders, navigators, and unreliable outsiders
- difficult to govern and impossible to pin down
- evasive but strangely perceptive
- a people whose songs know more than their leaders admit

## 3.4 Internal Truth

Thiên Lữ and The Eliza were once branches of the same old interpretive tradition during the second age.
Before they became opposites, both emerged from a shared culture that tried to read Ether through sky-patterns, ritual light, memory practice, and crisis guidance.

They split when that ancient tradition divided over a central question:

> Must light be followed through journey, or gathered into authority?

Thiên Lữ chose movement.
They refused to let revelation become institution.

## 3.5 What They Get Right

- living cultures preserve what dead archives cannot
- memory needs adaptation to survive long centuries
- fixed authority is not the same as truth
- patterns in sky, migration, and rhythm reveal changes before machines do
- some knowledge must be carried relationally, not owned centrally

## 3.6 What They Get Wrong

- they can avoid power even when intervention is necessary
- they often hide crucial knowledge in layered symbolism
- their resistance to centralization sometimes weakens collective action
- they may mistake poetic ambiguity for wisdom

## 3.7 Society and Social Structure

Thiên Lữ should feel like a mobile civilization, not a collection of random wanderers.
They move in **great caravans, walking courts, beast-cities, and song-lines**.

### Key roles
- **Star Readers** — interpreters of sky cycles, Ether drift, and route omens
- **Song Weavers** — memory custodians who encode history in melody, cadence, and layered verse
- **Trail Wardens** — protectors of caravan movement and route sanctity
- **Beast Keepers** — handlers of great migratory carriers and ceremonial animals
- **Veil Speakers** — diplomats, negotiators, interpreters of taboo and old vows

Authority in Thiên Lữ is portable and relational.
It depends on trust, memory, route legitimacy, and interpretive skill.

## 3.8 Daily Life

Thiên Lữ life should feel communal, mobile, rhythmic, and full of practical beauty.

Common features:
- caravan camps that bloom overnight and vanish by dawn
- layered textiles, hanging charms, tied memory cords, portable altars, light-filtering fabrics
- children learning routes, songs, constellations, and survival rituals early
- cooking, repair, divination, storytelling, and negotiation taking place in one shared flow
- memory preserved in pattern, refrain, knotting, and procession order

Their movement is not rootlessness.
It is a different form of rootedness.

## 3.9 Relationship to Ether

Thiên Lữ sees Ether as:
- a current that touches sky, body, memory, and event
- something listened to more than extracted
- a pattern that can be followed through drift, omen, beast movement, and dream

They do not reject practical Ether use, but they are deeply wary of over-concentration and forced containment.

## 3.10 Relationship to The Mist

Thiên Lữ has some of the earliest warnings that Ether is weakening.
Their routes, stars, and songs all show that the outer boundary is changing.

They often detect shifts in The Mist before settled powers do, which is one reason they are seen as prophetic.

## 3.11 Relationship to Donny Ivan

Thiên Lữ remembers Ivan in corrupted and symbolic forms:
- the Face Behind the Fire
- the King Who Tried to Nail Light to One Place
- the Ruler Who Mistook a World for a Throne

They do not preserve him as a founder, but as an example of what happens when revelation is claimed as ownership.

## 3.12 Relationship to Minh Yun

Thiên Lữ may preserve some of the most subtle traces of Minh Yun through songs and route-lore.
He may survive in their oral tradition as:
- the Listener of Fallen Stars
- the Quiet Hand Who Measured the Light
- the One Who Knew but Could Not Stop the Burning

His legacy may be encoded in lullabies, caravan rites, or half-forgotten warning songs.

## 3.13 Aesthetic Direction

### Shape language
- layered drapery
- asymmetrical movement
- curved weapons
- hanging talismans and tied objects
- crescent forms, eye motifs, star grids, mobile frames

### Materials
- woven cloth, bone, polished horn, faded metals, moonlike glass, portable lanterns, translucent beadwork
- lightweight gear designed for movement and repair

### Color direction
- night blue
- ash violet
- muted silver
- moon white
- pale turquoise
- sand gray
- star-gold as accent

## 3.14 Combat Identity

### Strategic role
- Speed striker / mobility DPS / pressure through rhythm and echo

### Combat fantasy
- uncatchable flow
- rapid repositioning
- momentum-based pressure
- layered afterimages, omen strikes, and cumulative tempo

Thiên Lữ should feel like a faction that becomes more dangerous the longer it remains in motion.

## 3.15 Internal Conflicts

- whether ancient songs should finally be decoded plainly as Ether crisis worsens
- conflict between route-preservers and interventionists
- whether to reveal the shared ancestral link with Eliza
- disputes over how much symbolic knowledge should be translated for outsiders
- generational rifts between traditional caravans and younger, more direct truth-seekers

## 3.16 Character Archetypes

- the caravan singer whose lullaby contains a map to a buried vault
- the star-reader who realizes old prophecies are really data patterns
- the route guardian forced to protect an Eliza defector
- the young wanderer who wants to stop running and build something permanent
- the memory-weaver who knows Thiên Lữ and Eliza were once one people

## 3.17 Writing Tone

Thiên Lữ dialogue should feel:
- poetic but not vague for its own sake
- precise in image rather than technical vocabulary
- rich in metaphor about sky, movement, cloth, hunger, distance, and return

They should speak like people used to remembering in layers.

## 3.18 Common Sayings

- “A road forgotten becomes a wound.”
- “Stars do not stay for walls.”
- “Carry what must live.”
- “A song tied too tightly stops breathing.”

## 3.19 Quest Hooks

- a lullaby known across three caravans is revealed to encode Minh Yun’s emergency warning system
- Thiên Lữ elders debate whether to expose the truth that Eliza once shared their root tradition
- a caravan route long considered cursed is actually an old Ether failure corridor reopening
- a star-reading points to a coming breach where two stable zones will collapse into one Mist front

# 4. THE ELIZA

## 4.1 Core Fantasy

**The long-lived church of sanctified order.**
A civilization of white stone, red veils, holy doctrine, institutional memory, aesthetic purity, and righteous violence.
They are not simply zealots.
They are the heirs to a sacred machine built over centuries from fear, discipline, revelation, and deliberate distortion.

## 4.2 Core Theme

> If truth can save the world, then it must be guarded, interpreted, and enforced.

The Eliza believes humanity cannot survive chaos without sacred authority.
Its greatest sin is turning fear of collapse into permanent justification for domination.

## 4.3 Public Image

Other civilizations often see The Eliza as:
- radiant and terrifying
- pious but manipulative
- beautiful, severe, and bloodstained
- keepers of holy order who too easily call dissent impurity
- a civilization that confuses obedience with salvation

## 4.4 Internal Truth

The Eliza is not a single-time invention.
It is the present form of a **continuous sacred tradition** stretching back through many eras of reinterpretation.

Its oldest roots lie near the aftermath of catastrophe and the memory of Golden Face.
Its deeper ideological root lies in the same ancient interpretive current that also produced Thiên Lữ.

Where Thiên Lữ chose journey,
The Eliza chose center.
Where Thiên Lữ kept revelation alive in motion,
The Eliza fixed revelation into hierarchy, scripture, architecture, and law.

## 4.5 What They Get Right

- humans need meaning, not only survival
- institutions preserve continuity across chaos
- ritual can stabilize communities under existential pressure
- beauty, discipline, and shared belief can hold civilizations together
- not every truth can be released carelessly in a fractured world

## 4.6 What They Get Wrong

- they treat authority as proof of legitimacy
- they sanctify distortion when it becomes useful
- they suppress alternative memory traditions
- they weaponize purity language
- they mistake divine order for their own institutional permanence

## 4.7 Society and Social Structure

The Eliza should feel old, ceremonial, educated, and severe.
Their civilization is not only clergy and soldiers.
It includes common believers, artisans, scribes, orphans, laborers, architects, and hidden dissenters.

### Key strata
- **The High Veil** — senior theologians, doctrinal rulers, interpreters of sacred legitimacy
- **The Scarlet Orders** — militant arms, inquisitorial branches, holy escorts, frontier purifiers
- **The White Choirs** — liturgical keepers, public ritual leaders, memory singers of canonized history
- **The Lamp Houses** — orphanages, schooling centers, training wards, scriptoria, social welfare arms
- **The Glass Sanctums** — relic vaults, scripture archives, sealed chambers of prohibited knowledge

Their power comes from combining spiritual dependency, social service, education, architecture, and sanctioned force.

## 4.8 Daily Life

The Eliza daily world should feel ordered, beautiful, and morally pressurized.

Common features:
- bells, processions, formal prayers, doctrine lessons, confession systems
- white stone avenues, colored glass, candle halls, altar machinery, relic shrines
- civic care attached to religious obedience
- charity distributed through supervised piety
- children taught reverence, discipline, liturgical response, and fear of outer corruption

Their world must feel genuinely sustaining to many people, not only oppressive.
That is what makes it powerful.

## 4.9 Relationship to Ether

The Eliza interprets Ether as sacred light, divine allowance, and proof that humanity remains under a higher order.
They are willing to use Ether technologically, but prefer to frame it ritually, symbolically, and doctrinally.

Some inner circles may know more than they admit about its unstable, worldly origins.

## 4.10 Relationship to The Mist

The Mist is interpreted as both punishment and boundary.
In Eliza theology, it may be called:
- the outer veil
- the dark flood of impurity
- the pressure beyond grace

As Ether weakens and breaches multiply, hardline branches within Eliza become more militant, claiming the world is entering an age of final testing.

## 4.11 Relationship to Donny Ivan

Golden Face is the theological transfiguration of Ivan.
He is not remembered as a political leader, but as the radiant source who led humanity out of annihilation and into ordered existence.

However, the audience must always understand the tension:
- The Eliza is wrong to call him a creator-god.
- Yet, in a literal historical sense, Ivan did shape the current world.

This should make Eliza doctrine tragically distorted rather than merely foolish.

## 4.12 Relationship to Minh Yun

The Eliza may preserve some of the most heavily censored traces of Minh Yun.
He may exist in forbidden records as:
- the First Profaner
- the False Witness of Light
- the Nameless Technician Before Revelation

Or in more hidden circles, as someone whose writings threaten the core theology of Golden Face.

## 4.13 Aesthetic Direction

### Shape language
- verticality, symmetry, processional form, layered veils, cathedral silhouettes, spear-like lines
- circles of radiance and severe triangular accents

### Materials
- pale stone, silver, gold, enamel, silk, stained glass, polished brass, relic steel
- sacred light reflected through architecture

### Color direction
- pure white
- blood red
- pale gold
- silver
- jewel-light accents in liturgical contexts

## 4.14 Combat Identity

### Strategic role
- Burst damage / faith buffer / risky zeal engine

### Combat fantasy
- holy aggression
- righteous pressure
- sacrificial power scaling
- radiant offense at the price of defense and restraint

They should feel like a faction whose certainty becomes force.

## 4.15 Internal Conflicts

- reformists who believe Golden Face should be reinterpreted as a warning, not a deity
- archivists hiding pre-doctrinal records
- power struggles between pastoral and militant branches
- fracture over how to respond to increasing Mist breaches
- buried unease over ancient links to Thiên Lữ traditions

## 4.16 Character Archetypes

- the young inquisitor whose faith cracks after reading a forbidden archive
- the choir scholar who discovers Eliza and Thiên Lữ once shared sacred forms
- the frontier priest genuinely saving villages while spreading harsher doctrine
- the orphan raised in Lamp Houses who knows no world outside Eliza mercy
- the high canonist who may know Golden Face was a man and chooses silence

## 4.17 Writing Tone

Eliza dialogue should feel:
- elevated but controlled
- ceremonial
- morally charged
- rich in language of purity, burden, grace, witness, order, judgment, and guardianship

Hardliners should sound certain.
Soft reformers should sound sorrowfully precise.

## 4.18 Common Sayings

- “What is pure must be kept.”
- “Light without order blinds.”
- “Mercy is not the absence of judgment.”
- “The veil holds because we do.”

## 4.19 Quest Hooks

- a hidden chapel contains pre-Eliza sky diagrams matching Thiên Lữ route symbols
- an inner archive suggests Golden Face was once treated as a sovereign, not a god
- a border bishop orders purges after Mist breaches increase, but the real cause is Ether lattice decay below the city
- an Eliza reform faction seeks the player’s help to recover records before hardliners burn them

# 5. THE SCAVENGER

## 5.1 Core Fantasy

**The civilization of ruin-profit and sharpened survival.**
A harsh port-and-junk culture built from wreckage, toxic adaptation, salvage law, weaponized reputation, and the economy of dangerous things.

The Scavenger is not just chaos.
It is a society that learned to live where normal order had already failed.

## 5.2 Core Theme

> If the world is broken, then survival belongs to those who can use what remains.

The Scavenger distrusts purity, permanence, and moral posturing.
Its virtue is adaptability.
Its sin is turning everything — relics, loyalty, memory, even people — into transaction.

## 5.3 Public Image

Other factions often see The Scavenger as:
- violent opportunists
- black-market kings of the ruins
- scavengers of poisoned coast and dead ports
- untrustworthy brokers and brutal duelists
- a people impossible to fully civilize or fully destroy

## 5.4 Internal Truth

The Scavenger emerged from coastal and harbor survivors shaped by toxic zones, wreck economies, and long exposure to dangerous relic spaces.
They are descendants of people who did not have the luxury of clean myth.
They lived among ship carcasses, broken depots, leaking industrial graveyards, and contaminated trade corridors.

Their “samurai” image should not be written as random aesthetic excess.
It should be rooted in ritualized duel culture, salvage authority, face-masks, oath marks, and port-guild honor systems warped by scarcity.

They are not lawless.
They obey **their own law of usable survival**.

## 5.5 What They Get Right

- ruins are still power
- high ideals often hide appetites
- survival requires flexibility and nerve
- relic economies shape the future whether anyone likes it or not
- those who live closest to danger often see reality first

## 5.6 What They Get Wrong

- they often turn cynicism into identity
- trust is too easily commodified
- brutality becomes self-reinforcing culture
- short-term gain can sabotage long-term survival
- they may help destroy the very frontier that feeds them

## 5.7 Society and Social Structure

The Scavenger should feel like a confederation of port-guilds, salvage houses, duel clans, and toxic-zone brokers.

### Key groups
- **Hull Houses** — major salvage families controlling ship carcasses, cranes, and harbor lanes
- **Mask Orders** — duel lineages, mercenary retainers, enforcers of port law
- **Scrap Brokers** — dealers of relics, components, maps, contraband, beast parts
- **Tide Riggers** — engineers of improvised machinery, lifts, filters, patched engines, and storm gear
- **Name-Takers** — witnesses, contract keepers, and punishers who record debts, betrayals, and sanctioned duels

Their law should feel brutal but functional.
Chaos is their image.
Controlled threat is their reality.

## 5.8 Daily Life

The Scavenger daily world should feel loud, improvised, dangerous, and economically alive.

Common features:
- stacked shacks built into ship skeletons and port towers
- mask stalls, duel rings, salvage lifts, toxic baths, repair pits, black kitchens
- children growing up among hooks, rust, rumors, and bargaining language
- contracts, threats, jokes, and debts flowing together in public life
- ritualized displays of nerve and competence as social currency

Their settlements should feel like they would collapse tomorrow — yet somehow never do.

## 5.9 Relationship to Ether

The Scavenger sees Ether as:
- valuable
- unstable
- tradable
- dangerous in all the ways that make profit possible

They are less interested in purity of use than in utility.
This makes them excellent smugglers of forbidden Ether devices and relic components.

## 5.10 Relationship to The Mist

The Scavenger lives closest to thresholds.
Many of their ports, hull graveyards, and sea routes sit near regions where The Mist can be watched, exploited, or survived for money.

They know more practical truths about Mist-beasts, edge-weather, salvage corridors, and breach patterns than most respectable powers do.

## 5.11 Relationship to Donny Ivan

Scavenger memory of Ivan is fragmented and cynical.
He may appear in sayings as:
- the Gold-Faced Burner
- the First Rich Man to Set the Sea on Fire
- the Lord Who Bought Tomorrow With Corpses

Unlike Eliza, they do not worship him.
Unlike Đế Quốc, they do not institutionalize him.
They reduce him to a brutal lesson: great men still leave scrap.

## 5.12 Relationship to Minh Yun

Minh Yun may survive here through relic signatures, old stamped components, broken lab crates, and market legends about a lost engineer whose marks still appear on impossible devices.

Scavenger traders might possess pieces of the truth without understanding their scale.

## 5.13 Aesthetic Direction

### Shape language
- jagged asymmetry
- layered patchwork
- hooks, straps, plates, hanging masks, improvised profiles
- compact forward-leaning silhouettes built for threat and motion

### Materials
- rusted steel, tire rubber, treated leather, plastic remnants, scavenged alloy, chain, stained cloth, improvised filters
- glowing toxic residue and jury-rigged Ether housings as accent

### Color direction
- burn-orange
- rust black
- toxic green
- faded jade
- oily silver
- bruised indigo in elite or ceremonial gear

## 5.14 Combat Identity

### Strategic role
- Melee assassin / burst duelist / evasion striker

### Combat fantasy
- brutal acceleration
- opportunistic punishment
- edge-of-death reflexes
- fast violence built on timing and nerve

They should feel dirty, clever, and thrillingly unsafe.

## 5.15 Internal Conflicts

- whether to remain profit-first as Ether collapse worsens
- old duel law versus new industrial crime syndicates
- whether frontier scavenging is awakening worse threats from beneath the ruins
- disputes between port stability and total market predation
- conflict between those who want legitimacy and those who thrive in perpetual volatility

## 5.16 Character Archetypes

- the masked duelist who enforces law no one else respects
- the salvage diver who found something under the hull fields that should not exist
- the black-market broker in possession of a Minh Yun-marked device
- the dock child rising into power through contracts and nerve
- the port chief trying to keep a trade city alive as Mist intrusions grow worse

## 5.17 Writing Tone

Scavenger dialogue should feel:
- sharp
- streetwise
- performative when public, precise when dealing business
- full of barter language, insults, threat-humor, and practical observation

They should sound like people who do not trust clean hands.

## 5.18 Common Sayings

- “If it still cuts, it still sells.”
- “A clean promise is worth checking twice.”
- “Rust remembers every owner.”
- “The sea gives nothing back for free.”

## 5.19 Quest Hooks

- a Scavenger market auction offers a sealed crate carrying Minh Yun’s mark and Eliza confiscation sigils
- duel law is invoked over control of a harbor that sits above a widening subterranean breach
- a port survives by buying monster parts from Mist hunters, but those hunts are drawing something larger inland
- an old hull-field contains records proving Ivan-era fleet routes once extended beyond today’s known stable zones

# 6. Faction Knowledge Matrix

## Đế Quốc knows best:
- infrastructural Ether systems
- large-scale logistics and applied engineering
- archival fragments of old technical civilization

## Thiên Lữ knows best:
- route memory
- predictive patterns
- ancient symbolic continuity
- early warnings in cycles and movement

## The Eliza knows best:
- continuity of doctrine across centuries
- hidden vaults, sanctified archives, old ceremonial sites
- the political force of belief

## The Scavenger knows best:
- relic circulation
- edge-zone survival
- practical realities of the Mist frontier
- black-market truths respectable powers cannot openly touch

No faction should ever know everything.
The main story grows stronger when major truth requires crossing faction boundaries.

# 7. Tone Guardrails

## Do not write any faction as purely correct.
Every major civilization preserves something necessary and distorts something dangerous.

## Avoid flattening factions into aesthetics.
Each faction must feel able to produce:
- farmers
- children
- teachers
- artisans
- cowards
- idealists
- opportunists
- lovers
- traitors
- saints
- monsters

## Keep the world tragic, not cynical.
The world is harsh because people inherited a wounded reality.
Not because nothing matters.

## Preserve ambiguity around Ivan.
He must remain interpretable from multiple moral positions.
The player should never feel forced into one simplistic conclusion.

## Preserve fragmentation around Minh Yun.
He should feel like a buried counter-history reassembling itself piece by piece.
